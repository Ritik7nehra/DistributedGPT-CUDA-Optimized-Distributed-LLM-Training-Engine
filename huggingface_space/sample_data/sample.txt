distributed-gpt is a small systems project built to study how efficiently a language model can be trained and how that training scales from one processor to many. The model is intentionally modest and built from scratch.

Training begins on a single device. A tokenizer turns raw text into integers. A dataset creates overlapping next-token examples. A small transformer predicts the next token, and gradient descent updates its weights.

Once the single-device loop works, the project asks efficiency questions: does more data-loader parallelism help, does lower precision change throughput, and what happens when multiple accelerators synchronize their gradients.

Distributed data parallel training replicates the model and averages gradients across workers. Fully sharded training divides model state across devices so larger workloads can fit.

Beyond one machine, SLURM schedules jobs across nodes and a fast network carries distributed communication. Checkpoints make long jobs resumable.

Finally, a FastAPI inference service loads a trained checkpoint and turns a prompt into generated text. The project connects learning, optimization, distribution, measurement and deployment in one system.
